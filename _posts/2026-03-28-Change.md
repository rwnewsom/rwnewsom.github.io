In 1996 John P. Kotter wrote “Leading Change”, a book which offers a formula for successfully leading organizations as
they adapt to new market conditions and disruptive technologies.  The book is so widely regarded it is on the reading
list of the Army Chief of Staff.  Not surprisingly, the book is also canon at a number of schools of business.  His
principles are at times elucidated through fictional or cinematic allegory, and that is the purpose of this work.
This post will compare it to the challenges faced by the antagonist in Milton’s epic poem, “Paradise Lost.”
These will be examined in the order of Kotter’s eight step model. Milton’s poem essentially started with an epic war in
Heaven.  Viewing the conflict through the lens of the business world we can see that war as a proxy fight, with God,
the son, and the loyal angels as the incumbent leadership and aligned shareholders.  Opposing them were Satan, his
assistants, and the angels / ‘shareholders’ he has recruited to his cause.  As with most actual proxy fights, the
incumbents shareholders won, and the consequences for the challengers were described as follows:

> Nine times the Space that measures Day and Night / To mortal men, he with his horrid crew
> Lay vanquisht, rolling in the fiery Gulf / Confounded though immortal: But his doom
> Reserv’d him to more wrath;...

Most challengers faced the prospect of lost time and profit if his proxy fight failed.
Satan and his minions were cast out of paradise and into a place of eternal torment.
This leads us to the first step of Kotter’s change model:
Step One: Establishing a Sense of Urgency.

A sense of urgency can be engineered to facilitate change in anticipation of future challenges.
When a company is on death’s door, however, this step will take care of itself.

For Satan and the fallen angels in his company the sense of urgency was also palpable.  It didn’t come from the
threat of layoffs.  Rather, it manifested in their new environment, which Satan beheld as follows:

>	At once as far as Angel’s ken he views / The dismal Situation waste and wild,
>	A Dungeon horrible, on all sides round / As one great Furnace flam’d, yet from those flames
>	No light, but rather darkness visible / Serv’d only to discover sights of woe...

If a state of eternal suffering doesn’t create a sense of urgency, I don’t know what will.
Here we reach the second step: Create the guiding coalition.

In all the Holy Scriptures or myths of the world, few individuals match Kotter’s proverbial
“highly visible individual” like Satan.  Yet even he, with all of his arrogance, acknowledged that he alone could
not affect change.  Therefore he set to creating a coalition, starting with his understandably demoralized
lieutenant, Beëlzebub.  In doing so, he simultaneously began work on the third step of Kotter’s model: Developing a
Vision and Strategy.  Satan initiated steps two and three by responding to the lamentations of Beëlzebub as follows:

>	Fall’n Cherub, to be weak is miserable / Doing or Suffering: but of this be sure,
>	To do aught good never will be our task, / But ever to do ill our sole delight,
>	As being contrary to his high will / Whom we resist...

Satan goes on, but truthfully Milton’s prose confounds even the most literate English major’s capacity to understand
Satan’s vision in five minutes or less.  We can forgive this deviation from Kotter’s model by acknowledging that
angelic entities grasp ‘wordy’ writing more readily than most employees we are apt to interact with.
The gist is that since they can’t conquer heaven, they will devote themselves to corrupting mankind
and creating a hell on earth.

  With the others brought in step, we can proceed to the fourth step: Communicating the Change Vision.

In order to Communicate the Change Vision, Satan gathered the leaders to discuss the future of his enterprise.
He asked all present to speak their minds, and heard from four of his lieutenants.  First Moloch spoke, who advised
open war.  Then Belial spoke, and counseled against war, lest their torments be increased.  Mammon seconded this.
Then Beëlzebub spoke, and noted that while both open war and submission to passive damnation were both untenable,
a third option existed:

>	There is a place… another World, the happy seat / Of some new Race call’d Man…here perhaps
>	Some advantageous act may be achiev’d… / To waste his whole Creation, or possess
>	All as our own, and drive as we were driven, / The puny inhabitants, or if not drive,
>	Seduce them to our Party…

In this case Beëlzebub was able to communicate the vision on Satan’s behalf.  It was quickly understood and embraced
be the ‘employees’:  “The bold design / Pleas’d highly those infernal States, and joy / Sparkl’d in all their eyes…”

Empowering Employees for Broad-Based Action is the 5th step in Kotter’s model.  In Satan’s case this involved the
removal of the old angelic hierarchy.  While in Heaven, they were ordered Cherubim, Thrones, Dominions, Virtues,
Powers, Principalities, Archangels and lastly, Angels.
(Note that none of the Seraphim – the highest ranked in the order, had rebelled).
In Hell they were free of that cast system, the former titles mentioned only to remind them of more noble origins.
Removing such ‘structural barriers’ would make ‘implementation of the change vision’ possible.

The sixth step of Kotter’s model is Generating Short-Term Wins.  Satan recognized the need for a short-term win,
and lined up two.  The first was escaping hell and entering paradise.
Hell had been prepared by almighty God to serve as a place of eternal damnation, so this was not an easy prospect.
Upon reaching the nine gates of hell, he found them not only barred and locked, but guarded by two fearsome
creatures: Sin and Death.  Facing Death, the aggressor of the two (and also his descendent, but I digress),
Satan had this to say:

>	Whence and what art thou, execrable shape, / That dar’st, though grim and terrible, advance
>	Thy miscreated Front athwart my way / To yonder Gates? Through them I mean to pass,
>	That be assur’d, without leave askt of thee: / Retire, or taste thy folly, and learn by proof,
>	Hell-born, not to contend with Spirits of Heav’n.

Satan thus got through the gates and beheld Eden.  As for the second quick win, for Satan it was tempting humanity
and getting them cast out of paradise. These gains will give the infernal company élan, but to sustain it the gains
must be consolidated.

The seventh step is Consolidating Gains and Producing More Change. Kotter cautions against glad-handing early
success because it breeds complacency. Things certainly seemed to be going Hell’s way.  Satan found that Sin and
Death and built a bridge to the new world, making future crossings even easier.  His minions were proud of his
accomplishments, as Milton writes:

>   Their mighty Chief return’d: loud was th’acclaim:
>   Forth rushed in haste the great consulting Peers, / Rais’d from their dark Divan, and with like joy
>   Congratulant approach’d him, who with hand / Silence, and with these words attention won.

Satan proceeded to give a speech, further extolling his success, but not giving his company any future direction or
guidance. The company must be streamlined, and new customers sought.

The eighth and final step in Kotter’s model is Anchoring New Approaches in the Culture.  This step came quite
involuntarily for Satan and his damned crew.  As he finished his self congratulatory address he was met not by applause,
but by hissing.  Transformation into serpents – the form Satan used to tempt humanity - irrevocably anchored change into
the culture of the fallen angels.

>   His Visage drawn he felt to sharp and spare, / His Arms clung to his Ribs, his Legs entwining
>   Each other, till supplanted down he fell / A monstrous Serpent on his Belly prone,
>   Reluctant, but in vain, a greater power / Now rul’d him, punisht in the shape he sinn’d…


TLDR:  Beware the temptation of complacency.
